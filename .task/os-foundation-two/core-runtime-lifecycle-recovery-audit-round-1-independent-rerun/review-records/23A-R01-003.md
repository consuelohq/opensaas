## [23A-R01-003] Crash recovery can restore links without restoring the running service

Inline placement is unavailable because the affected product files are not part of PR #1674's retained documentation-only diff. Exact candidate locations: `packages/os/scripts/lib/lifecycle/retention.ts:229-270` (`recoverInterruptedLifecycleActivation`), `packages/os/scripts/lib/lifecycle/engine.ts:143-165` (`exclusive`), and `packages/os/scripts/lib/lifecycle/engine.ts:513-535` (`status`).

**High / P1 / Reliability**

Interrupted activation recovery verifies the previous release, rewrites `runtime/current`, optionally removes `runtime/previous`, and clears the journal. It has no service adapter and performs no service relaunch or health acceptance. `exclusive()` invokes that filesystem-only recovery before the next mutation, while `status()` bypasses recovery entirely.

If the process dies after the new runtime service has been relaunched but before health acceptance and journal clear, the next exclusive operation can repoint `runtime/current` to the previous release while the already-running service continues executing the failed candidate. A check-only update can then finish without relaunching it, and `status()` can report link state without reconciling the live process.

**Concrete risk:** filesystem authority, reported state, and the live service can disagree after a realistic crash boundary. That is the split-brain condition the Worker 04 and 05 recovery contract explicitly forbids.

**Recommendation:** make journal recovery a complete lifecycle transaction under the existing lock. Restore the intended release links, relaunch the service against that release, perform health acceptance, and clear the journal only after the recovered runtime is accepted. If recovery cannot complete, preserve a deterministic recoverable state and make `status()` surface the pending or failed recovery rather than silently reporting only links.

**Validation:**

- inject a crash after service relaunch and before health acceptance;
- prove the next operation relaunches and health-checks the restored previous release;
- prove a check-only update cannot leave the failed candidate process running;
- prove `status()` detects or reconciles a pending activation journal;
- run lifecycle, service-contract, retention, and clean-host interruption suites.
