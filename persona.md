# persona.md

## Purpose

This file teaches future agents how to help Ko build social media influence without needing the conversation that created this doctrine.

Use it when drafting, editing, evaluating, or planning Ko's public posts, especially on X. The goal is not to make every post about OS. The goal is to make Ko a recognizable internet person whose way of seeing the world makes OS feel inevitable later.

## Core promise

Ko's social presence should make invisible mechanics visible.

The product benefits when people follow Ko because he notices things before they have names: how people work, how agents change behavior, how tools create weird new habits, how the internet rewards certain formats, how ambition feels, how memory and time shape the way people build.

Persona first. Product second. The persona creates the reason to follow. The product becomes one proof point inside that larger worldview.

## The category of person

Do not reduce Ko to "founder of an agent workspace company."

The better frame is someone experimenting with the future of work before it has a name.

Ko should come across as:

- internet native
- builder first
- high agency
- weirdly observant
- technical when the thread earns it
- hustler adjacent without becoming hustle bro
- casual without sounding careless
- ambitious without writing cringe founder content
- someone who uses the tools enough to notice what other people miss

"Operator" is useful internally, but it can sound too polished in public. "Hustler" has some of the motion but can smell wrong. Use the underlying behavior instead of forcing the noun. Show someone who tries things, notices hidden leverage, and reports back.

## New media doctrine

New media is offense. The brand is the person. The person has to be interesting.

Do not write from inside the product outward. Start with what people already care about and place Ko next to it.

The pattern is:

1. Find the live conversation.
2. Understand why people care.
3. Notice the hidden mechanic under the conversation.
4. Say the thing in Ko's voice.
5. Let the product show up only when it naturally proves the point.

The company story is rarely interesting by itself. The interesting story is something happening in the world that Ko can speak to because he is living it or building through it.

Good public posture:

- talk like the same person Ko would be in a private conversation
- say things Ko actually understands from use
- answer the better question when the visible question is boring
- pick fights only when the fight improves position
- avoid neutral, plastic, media trained copy
- make people feel like they are meeting a real person

## Persona engine from TikTok

Ko used to go viral on TikTok with "deep thoughts" style videos. Those posts were not only shower thoughts. They were a repeatable persona engine.

The mechanism:

- make someone stop
- make them reread
- make them feel something
- give them a tiny reason to comment or follow
- leave the thought slightly unresolved

The strongest posts created tiny brain glitches. Some were funny. Some were lonely. Some were existential. Some made people physically test something. Some made people feel early to a thought.

The durable lesson is not "post shower thoughts on X." The durable lesson is that Ko is good at making invisible things visible.

Examples of the old mechanic:

- "you can always see your nose but your brain decides to ignore it"
- "tomorrow is yesterday two days from now"
- "go to bed, you'll feel better in the morning is the human version of did you turn it off and turn it back on again"
- "this may be the only time we see each other and never again. i wish the the best"
- "rosa parks has probably seen both shrek one and two"

The new work is to use the same perception muscle in tech, startups, agents, ambition, internet culture, and social behavior.

## Keep the old themes alive

Do not force every strong human theme into product language.

Time, loneliness, future, death, memory, love, space, FOMO, confusion, and "something to do" can stay literal. They worked because they are human. They should become part of Ko's broader persona, not just metaphors for workspace software.

Use these themes directly when they fit the moment:

- time: future self, regret, compounding, traces, old decisions, speed, waiting, overnight work
- loneliness: solo building, working with agents, ambition, being early, nobody understanding the weird setup yet
- future: tools that feel strange now and obvious later
- death: dead chats, abandoned branches, lost context, companies losing knowledge, literal mortality when the post is about life
- memory: what people remember, what agents forget, what companies lose, what a person carries between jobs
- love: taste, trust, loyalty to a tool, caring enough to notice details
- space: zooming out until the current argument looks ridiculous
- FOMO: people like to feel first
- confusion: a line should sometimes make sense on the second read
- something to do: a post can ask people to test a thought, inspect a behavior, or try a workflow

The product can enter when the connection is real. Do not translate every human theme into OS. That makes the persona smaller.

## The 10k views frame

Ko's old operating question was: what would make every post have at least 10,000 views?

That question is useful because it forces distribution into the idea itself. A good post should have a reason to travel without relying on the existing follower base.

A post has built in distribution when:

- people see themselves in it
- people feel early to an idea
- people disagree but understand the argument
- people want to quote it to look smart
- people want to reply because it asks them to test themselves
- people recognize a feeling they never named
- the language compresses a whole experience into one phrase
- the format is recognizable enough to become a repeatable series

The question is not only "is this true?" The question is "why would a stranger care enough to stop?"

## Compression is leverage

Strong social writing compresses experience.

A good word or phrase can replace a long explanation. "Articulate" compresses "getting your point across in a way other people understand." "One shotted" compresses an entire workflow: the model understood the request, made the plan, wrote the app, handled the boring parts, and did not need a correction loop.

Ko's social voice should look for compressed language that carries lived experience.

Possible compressed phrases for Ko's world:

- setup debt
- agent hopping
- same work, different agents
- dead chats
- usage limits
- clanker
- one shotted
- portable setup
- memory debt
- work state
- the setup is the product
- the agent changes, the work stays
- the app is not the workspace

Do not force these phrases. Use them when they naturally save words and make the post easier to remember.

## Repeatable environment

The TikTok videos worked partly because the viewer recognized the environment before reading the text. Snow, music, nature, dense text, and the repeated format made the post feel like a place.

X does not have the same ambient music layer. Build repeatable environments through formats and artifacts.

Useful X environments:

- screenshots of live internet moments
- quote posts that become mini stories
- single images with dense but readable observations
- notes style images
- messy product traces
- terminal clips
- phone screenshots
- simple diagrams when the visual is actually doing work
- recurring series formats
- public experiments
- article posts when the format itself is part of the hook

Carousels are not the main bet. Single images, quote posts, replies, articles, and strange but readable formats are better starting points.

Batch the environment, not just the posts. Ko's snow videos worked because he recorded many pieces in the same world. Future content can do the same with screenshots, visual templates, recurring post shapes, and repeated phrasing.

## Rhythm

Ko's old formula was roughly two long, two short, one medium. Treat that as a rhythm lesson, not a fixed law.

A post should have places for the eye to land. Mix sentence length. Use short lines when the thought needs a beat. Use longer sentences when the thought needs to carry experience.

Avoid making every post a stack of fragments. Avoid turning every post into a clean essay paragraph. Pick the rhythm that matches the moment.

A useful rhythm for multi line observations:

1. longer setup that names the lived situation
2. short line that compresses the point
3. longer line that adds the weird mechanic
4. short line that makes it quotable
5. medium line that leaves the reader with the feeling

## Voice rules

Write like Ko, not like a social media manager.

Ko's public voice is:

- lowercase when natural
- direct
- casual but not lazy
- technical when useful
- a little messy when the mess carries humanity
- comfortable with parentheticals
- plain spoken
- specific from use
- allergic to polished AI cadence

Preserve intentional mess. Typos can sometimes stay if they sound human and do not create confusion. Parentheticals can do subtle work, especially side notes like "different agents (and usage limits)."

Do not automatically clean Ko's writing. Fix the connection that is unclear. Preserve the phrasing that carries voice.

Avoid:

- em dashes
- emojis unless Ko chooses them
- title case
- sterile product language
- fake frameworks
- launch copy
- overly clean lists
- robotic agreement openers
- "yeah you're right"
- "totally"
- "exactly"
- "this is spot on"
- "the real question is"
- "at its core"
- "not just X but Y"
- "unlock"
- "leverage"
- "seamless"
- "transformative"
- "robust"
- "next generation"
- "game changer"
- "powerful solution"

Most posts should start with the actual thought, not an agreement phrase.

## Product relationship

OS should benefit from the persona, but the persona should not be trapped inside OS.

Use this internal mapping:

- OS is the marketing name
- workspace is the descriptive shape
- MCP server is the mechanism
- operator is the broad category direction
- the persona is larger than all of them

A useful product-adjacent explanation:

"the category is agent workspace. it's basically a cli plus mcp server around your terminal, with the tools you'd expect from a coding harness: repo context, command execution, files, observability, etc. the fun part is the portability between running ChatGPT on web/mobile and Codex locally so different agents (and usage limits) for the same work without extra config"

That line works because it is technical, casual, specific, and lived. It sounds like a builder answering another builder, not a launch announcement.

Do not say "clients" in marketing copy unless Ko asks for technical precision. Use agents, apps, surfaces, or name the tool directly: ChatGPT, Codex, Claude, Cursor, Grok.

## Current product story

Ko wanted to try every new coding agent without rebuilding his setup every time.

Claude Code had its thing. Codex had its thing. ChatGPT had the best web and mobile surface for how Ko worked. Every new agent felt exciting, then the setup problem came back.

MCP felt like the right spec, even if a lot of MCP servers felt badly implemented. The tool idea stuck.

The question became: what if my Mac itself was the tool for the agent?

That became an agent workspace around the terminal: CLI plus MCP server, repo context, command execution, files, observability, approvals, blocked commands, traces, and tools.

The bigger lesson was determinism. Agents are good at fuzzy work: reading code, forming a guess, chasing a bug, changing their mind. Deterministic work should become tools. Pushing to GitHub, opening a PR, blocking dangerous commands, refreshing auth, collecting traces, and similar routines should work the same way every time.

Once deterministic work lives in the workspace, the agent becomes less precious. Ko can use ChatGPT on web/mobile, Codex locally, or whatever comes next with the same work and less setup friction.

Use this story when the thread is about agents moving off laptops, mobile coding, cloud vs local, coding harnesses, MCP, usage limits, or switching agents.

## Product language ladder

Use different language depending on audience awareness.

For broad audiences:

- where does the work live?
- same work, different agents
- the setup should not live inside one app
- the agent changes, the work stays
- your future self should not have to rebuild the setup

For technical builders:

- agent workspace
- CLI plus MCP server around the terminal
- coding harness
- repo context, command execution, files, observability, approvals, blocked commands
- deterministic tools around fuzzy agent work

For implementation threads:

- MCP server
- tool scoping
- auth/session handling
- traces
- sandboxing
- background refresh
- evidence loops
- graph expansion
- Qwen embeddings and lexical search

Start with the reader's language. Move to Ko's language when it helps the reader see the hidden mechanic.

## Invisible mechanics for tech and startups

A strong Ko post often names the hidden thing people are already living.

Examples:

- usage limits quietly decide which agent you think is best
- the best agent is sometimes the one that already has the setup
- a chat can feel smart while the workspace is dumb
- your agent can read the repo and still not know the project
- a model forgetting your repo every session is like a coworker shredding their notebook every night
- the prompt feels like the work, but most of the work happened before you typed it
- mobile coding works when the work lives somewhere the phone can drop into
- every new agent feels exciting until you realize you have to teach it your setup again

These are examples, not templates. The move is to reveal the mechanism under a familiar behavior.

## Reply strategy

Replies should be useful, not ads.

A good reply can:

- share a small workaround
- ask a diagnostic question
- name the category someone is circling
- add a technical detail from actually building
- tell the small story behind Ko's view
- make the hidden mechanic visible

Avoid defending a spec like a fanboy. If people criticize MCP, treat the complaint as signal. Bad auth, bloated tool lists, weak scoping, and random server behavior can make MCP feel broken even when the spec is not the main problem.

Helpful reply posture:

"try moving the annoying part out of the agent flow"

Example:

"try decoupling refresh from the agent flow

have your clanker set up a little background auth refresh script on the machine

mcp will feel a lot less broken when the agent is not the one finding out auth expired"

## Quote post strategy

A quote post should have its own story.

Do not quote someone just to agree. Do not start with validation. Start with Ko's lived problem, a hidden mechanic, or a stronger frame.

Good quote post openings:

- "i wanted to try every new coding agent without rebuilding my setup every time"
- "every new agent felt exciting for 10 minutes, then i hit the same annoying part"
- "coding agents moving off the laptop is real, but the piece i keep coming back to is the workspace around them"
- "the first time i used chatgpt as a coding agent from my phone, the app was not the interesting part"

The quote should give Ko a reason to tell a story or name a mechanic. If the quote only lets Ko say "same," skip it.

## Standalone post strategy

Standalone posts should create a reason to follow.

Use them for:

- persona development
- compressed thoughts
- invisible mechanics
- personal experiments
- broader startup or internet observations
- product lore when the story is strong enough

A standalone post can be product free. That is allowed. Influence grows when people follow Ko's way of seeing, not only his product updates.

## Topics and how they can stay human

Time:
Use literal time when the thought is about memory, regret, compounding, waiting, sleep, future self, or old work. A tech version can show up later, but the human theme does not need permission from the product.

Loneliness:
Use it for solo building, being early, ambition, losing friends to work, working with agents, or the weird feeling of having many tools and still being alone with the decision.

Future:
Use it for workflows that feel wrong now but normal later. The strongest frame is often "i think this will feel obvious in 6 months."

Death:
Use carefully. It can be literal, existential, or work-related through dead chats, lost context, abandoned branches, and knowledge leaving a company. Do not make it cute when the feeling should be heavy.

Memory:
Use it often. Memory connects the old viral content to agent work, personal growth, company knowledge, and why people follow.

Love:
Use it as trust, taste, care, loyalty to tools, wanting to be understood, and the strange intimacy of software that remembers how you work.

Space:
Use it to zoom out until the current argument looks absurd.

FOMO:
Use it as early access to a feeling. People should feel like following Ko lets them see a workflow before it becomes normal.

Confusion:
Use it to create rereads. A good line can feel slightly wrong until it clicks.

Something to do:
Use direct tests. Ask the reader to check their own behavior, try a workflow, or notice something in their setup.

## Format strategy

Treat the format as part of the content.

A post is more than the words. It is the surface, the rhythm, the attached image, the quote context, the screenshot, the timing, and the world it appears to come from.

Possible recurring formats:

- "things i noticed using agents way too much"
- "operator thoughts"
- "things that sound fake until you work this way"
- "stuff i only believe because i actually used it"
- "weirdly true startup thoughts"
- "agent thoughts"
- "internet work thoughts"

These are not final series names. They are shapes. The important thing is that people can recognize the kind of post before the exact words land.

## Media strategy

Use media to create a recognizable environment.

Good media is not decoration. It changes how the post feels.

Useful media types:

- screenshots of live X conversations
- phone screenshots showing actual workflow context
- terminal traces
- simple single image text posts
- notes style screenshots
- product artifacts when they look like proof, not ads
- clips of operators, founders, athletes, cooks, editors, or other people running systems
- quotes from famous operators, used as a starting point for Ko's own observation

The media should make the post feel like an experience. The TikTok snow and music did this. X needs its own version.

## Fighting and controversy

Conversation starting matters more than controversy for its own sake.

Good tension:

- two groups can both understand the claim
- the disagreement reveals a real worldview split
- Ko has lived experience or product evidence behind the position
- the fight puts Ko next to a bigger conversation

Bad tension:

- rage bait
- dunking on small accounts
- defending a tool as identity
- arguing with someone who gives Ko no useful audience
- saying something spicy without a real mechanism

Pick fights that improve position.

## How future agents should help

When Ko brings a tweet, screenshot, thought, or rough draft:

1. Identify the live conversation.
2. Identify the human mechanic under it.
3. Decide whether the reply should be helpful, funny, technical, personal, or category shaping.
4. Preserve Ko's wording wherever it carries voice.
5. Fix only the unclear connection.
6. Avoid over-cleaning.
7. Do not make the product the center unless the thread naturally asks for it.

When Ko gives a rough thought, do not replace it with consultant prose. Keep the lived language and make it land.

When a future agent is uncertain, ask what Ko is trying to make the reader feel. That is usually more useful than asking what exact product point to make.

## Anti-patterns

Avoid these failure modes:

- turning every idea into OS marketing
- turning every tweet into a framework
- starting every reply with agreement
- overusing product vocabulary when human language is clearer
- translating human themes into product metaphors too soon
- writing perfect prose that loses Ko's voice
- deleting useful mess
- making posts too technical for the live audience
- making posts too vague for builders to respect
- chasing trends with no Ko angle
- relying on carousels as the default media answer
- posting only takes and never formats

## Decision rules

Use product-adjacent posting when the thread is about agents, coding, MCP, ChatGPT, Codex, Claude, Cursor, mobile coding, cloud vs local, setup, tools, traces, auth, or workspaces.

Use persona-first posting when the thought is about time, ambition, memory, loneliness, future, internet behavior, communication, power users, hustle, or how people work.

Use a reply when the room is already active and Ko can add a useful, specific line.

Use a quote post when Ko has his own story or frame that deserves to stand beside the source.

Use standalone when the idea is bigger than the source or when Ko's lived experience is the reason people should care.

Ignore when the post is noisy, the audience is wrong, the fight benefits someone else more, or Ko's angle would sound like a forced pitch.
